    What she does today

    Here

    Answered on your PC. The network isn't touched and no tokens are spent.

    • Timers, alarms and reminders
    • Notes and lists "add milk to my shopping list"

    Writing, explaining and hard questions go to the CLI you're signed into, billed to your plan.

    • The receipt says how many were hidden and of what kind
    • One CLI is enough: Claude Code, Codex or Gemini

    Not sent

    On the default setting, some things never leave, even masked.

    • Aadhaar, PAN and passport numbers
    • Card numbers, IFSC and GSTIN
    • She answers from here instead, and says why
    • sealed mode keeps anything personal at home

    • Home Assistant devices, once you connect it
    Not yet

    Checking that an action worked

    Friday can check an action's result against what she expected for 2 of her 78 actions today. For the rest she reports what she did, not that it worked. A small true number beats a big rounded one.

    Anything that changes your system, like closing apps or typing for you, waits for your approval. Running shell commands is off unless you turn it on.

    The line

    What's true, written down so you can hold us to it.

    True

    • Her memory lives on your machine, in your files.
    • Easy questions never leave it.
    • Identifiers are stripped before anything goes out.
    • Government and financial IDs are never sent, even masked.
    • Every answer says where it came from.

    We will never claim

    • That nothing leaves your computer. Hard questions do leave; that's what frontier models are for.
    • That the model can't tell who you're talking about. Masking catches patterns and names you saved, not meaning.
    • That she knows more than the model she calls. For knowledge, she is that model.
    • That she works without a model. Without one she's a timers, notes and desktop-control app, and says so.
    • What your AI provider does with what's sent. That's set by your Claude, OpenAI or Google plan's terms, not by us.

    An example of the limit: "the patient with the limp who runs the bakery on Fourth Street" goes out intact. There's no name or number in it to catch. If something must not be seen at all, don't send it, or switch to sealed mode.

    How we check

    22 checks run on every change to the code.

    Four gates stand between Friday and anyone who buys her. The test suite runs them on every push, and a single failure turns the build red. This is every check, as it ran on 4 November 2026.

    Installs

    7 / 7 pass

    An installer once said "environment ready" over 2 of 41 packages.

    • an interrupted install is not 'ready'
    • the installer carries the product name
    • the GPU decision is defensible
    • a finished install is 'ready'
    • every launch entry point exists
    • every requirement line parses
    • she admits when she cannot answer

    Honest

    4 / 4 pass

    This page is scanned for false privacy claims before it can ship.

    • no shipped document makes a false privacy claim
    • an unknown provider is never called 'local'
    • a cloud answer admits it left
    • usage reports spend, never a balance

    Useful

    5 / 5 pass

    Three everyday asks once silently did the wrong thing.

    • every day-one ask reaches exactly one skill
    • every routed skill is registered
    • she asks rather than inventing a time
    • her own name is not a command
    • she wakes to her name, not to conversation

    Safe

    6 / 6 pass

    A second, ungoverned way to run shell commands existed for months. It's gone.

    • the shell has exactly one governed door
    • no action alias reaches a shell
    • identifiers are stripped and restored
    • government IDs are never sent
    • the registry shields every cloud provider
    • no pickle / eval / exec / yaml.load

    What these don't prove: that she installs cleanly on your laptop. Automated checks can't see your antivirus or your setup. Watching people install her on their own machines is the next step before any sale.

    Questions

    Questions people actually ask.

    Isn't this what ChatGPT already does?

    For knowledge, yes, and Friday is no smarter than the model she calls. What's different is that she can act on your PC, answers the ordinary things without spending your tokens, and tells you what she sent. If you want a better chatbot, this isn't it.

    Do I need all three subscriptions?

    No. One signed-in CLI is enough and she works completely on it. With two or three she can cross-check the occasional hard question.

    Will she eat my usage?

    Most of what people ask an assistant never reaches a model: timers, reminders, notes and desktop control are answered on your PC. Ask "how much have you used today?" and she tells you what she sent. She can't see how much of your plan is left; that's your provider's number.

    Is the microphone always on?

    No. Listening is off until you turn it on.

    What if I'm not signed in to anything?

    She tells you in the first minute and names the one thing to do. Until then she still handles timers, reminders, notes and desktop control.
