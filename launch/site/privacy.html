What stays on your computer

All of it. Her memory, your reminders, timers, notes and lists, your contacts, the name you chose for her, and her record of what she has sent. These are ordinary files in her data folder; you can read them, back them up or delete them, and nothing syncs anywhere.

Each person on a machine has their own account, and what Friday learns is kept under that account and tied to that install. One account never reads another's memory, and a data folder copied to a different device is not adopted as anyone's.

What leaves your computer, and when

This is the part most assistants are vague about, so here it is plainly.

  • Easy questions never leave. Reminders, timers, alarms, notes, lists, opening apps, volume, brightness, windows, files, the clock: answered on your machine, with the network untouched.
  • Hard questions do leave. They go to whichever AI you are signed into (Claude, Codex or Gemini) through the command-line tool you already pay for. Your question travels with them, and that company's privacy policy applies to it, not ours.
  • Before anything leaves, identifiers are stripped. Names in your contacts, phone numbers and email addresses are replaced with placeholders and restored on your machine afterwards. The mapping never leaves the process.
  • Some things are not sent at all. Aadhaar, PAN, passport, card, IFSC and GSTIN numbers that she recognises are withheld, even in masked form, because masking one still reveals that it exists. She answers from her own reasoning instead, and says so. She recognises them by their shape, so one written in an unusual way can be missed: today that includes digits split by dots, slashes or a line break, numbers spelled out in words or in full-width digits, and a PAN in lowercase.
  • Web searches leave. If you ask her to look something up she contacts the search engine or site directly, as your browser would.
  • Smart-home commands go to your own Home Assistant on your own network.

Every answer tells you which of these happened.

The honest limit

The stripping catches patterns and the names you have written down, not meaning. "The patient with the limp who runs the bakery on Fourth Street" goes out intact. So the truthful claim is that identifiers are stripped, never that the model cannot work out who someone is. If something must never be seen at all, do not send it: sealed mode keeps anything personal on your machine.

No tracking inside Friday

Friday has no analytics service, no telemetry and no licence check over the network. Crash reports are written to a file on your computer and stay there. Friday can send them somewhere only if you switch that on yourself and give it an address to send to; it is off when installed, and we run no such address. Her licence key is verified by a signature on your own machine. We cannot tell whether you have installed her, opened her, or stopped using her.
